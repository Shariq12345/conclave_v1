\section{Introduction}

The proliferation of Artificial Intelligence across sensitive domains—such as healthcare, medical diagnostics, financial fraud detection, and manufacturing—necessitates collaborative model training across multi-institutional datasets~\cite{rieke2020future}. Traditional machine learning relies on centralizing raw datasets, introducing critical vulnerabilities including catastrophic data breaches and non-compliance with statutory privacy laws such as the U.S. Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)~\cite{hipaa2003security} and EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)~\cite{gdpr2016regulation}.

Federated Learning (FL) enables collaborative model training without raw data centralization~\cite{mcmahan2017communication, kairouz2021advances}. Participating clients maintain local datasets, executing iterative optimization on local compute nodes and transmitting only model parameter updates (gradients or weights) to a central orchestrator.

\subsection{Motivation and Enterprise Governance Gaps}

Mainstream FL frameworks—such as Flower~\cite{beutel2020flower}, NVIDIA FLARE~\cite{roth2022nvidia}, TensorFlow Federated~\cite{kairouz2021advances}, OpenFL~\cite{foley2022openfl}, FedML~\cite{he2020fedml}, FATE~\cite{yang2019fate}, and PySyft~\cite{ryffel2018generic}—focus predominantly on model distribution, communication compression, and training efficiency. Consequently, they treat enterprise governance, regulatory compliance, access control, and auditable guarantees as out-of-scope concerns. In enterprise deployments, this gap presents critical barriers:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Absence of Dynamic Policy Enforcement:} Standard FL orchestrators lack mechanisms to enforce access rules, verify node compliance (e.g., TPM 2.0 / TEE attestation quotes~\cite{mo2021ppfl}, mTLS certificates), or map system operations to regulatory mandates (e.g., HIPAA \S164.312~\cite{hipaa2003security} and GDPR Articles 5, 7, 17, and 32~\cite{gdpr2016regulation}).
    \item \textbf{Inflexible Cryptographic and Privacy Control:} Unmasked updates are vulnerable to gradient inversion attacks~\cite{zhu2019deep, geiping2020inverting}. Existing FL deployments either omit privacy guarantees entirely or rely on basic additive masking that fails under client dropouts. They lack threshold secret sharing for dropout-resilient mask reconstruction~\cite{bonawitz2017practical, bell2020secagg}, as well as formal differential privacy budget accounting (R\'enyi DP~\cite{mironov2017renyi, balle2020hypothesis}) to track cumulative privacy leakage ($\epsilon, \delta$).
    \item \textbf{Lack of Immutable Auditability and Machine Unlearning:} Enterprise FL systems require audit trails to prove regulatory compliance. Furthermore, under GDPR Article 17 (Right to Erasure), FL systems must support participant revocation and machine unlearning protocols~\cite{liu2021federaser, bourtoule2021machine, wang2023federated} without corrupting global model state.
    \item \textbf{Adversarial Vulnerability under Skewed Distributions:} Real-world datasets exhibit severe statistical heterogeneity (non-IID data modeled via Dirichlet distributions $\operatorname{Dir}(\alpha)$)~\cite{hsu2019measuring, li2020federated}. Standard FedAvg degrades in accuracy and is vulnerable to Byzantine gradient poisoning attacks~\cite{blanchard2017machine, fang2020local}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Proposed Solution: FedGuard and Conclave}

To address the operational gap between distributed training and regulatory requirements, this paper presents \textbf{FedGuard}, a framework-agnostic governance control plane for Federated Learning. FedGuard establishes an independent operational governance layer enforcing identity verification, compliance validation, access control, privacy budget tracking, and immutable audit logging throughout the FL lifecycle.

To evaluate our proposed architecture, we implement \textbf{Conclave}, a reference implementation integrating FedGuard with Flower. Conclave features:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Threshold Secure Aggregation:} An ephemeral X25519 key agreement protocol with Shamir's Secret Sharing~\cite{bonawitz2017practical, so2021lightsecagg}, enabling surviving nodes to cancel dropped client masks without exposing active updates.
    \item \textbf{Formal Privacy Accounting:} A R\'enyi Differential Privacy accountant~\cite{mironov2017renyi} with Gaussian noise injection to track cumulative ($\epsilon, \delta$) privacy expenditure per organization.
    \item \textbf{Cryptographic Audit Ledger:} A SHA-256 hash-chained event log linking governance events, policy checks, and consent revocations.
    \item \textbf{Byzantine Resilience under Non-IID Data:} Integration of robust aggregators (Trimmed Mean, Median, Krum~\cite{blanchard2017machine, yin2018byzantine}) under Dirichlet non-IID partitioning ($\alpha=0.5$) for PyTorch ResNet-18 workloads.
\end{itemize}

Crucially, we do not claim novelty in individual mathematical or cryptographic primitives—such as ECDH key exchange~\cite{bonawitz2017practical}, Shamir secret sharing, RDP~\cite{mironov2017renyi}, TPM attestation~\cite{mo2021ppfl}, or robust aggregators~\cite{blanchard2017machine, yin2018byzantine}. Rather, our contribution lies in the architectural design, formalization, and evaluation of a unified control plane (FedGuard) that orchestrates these established mechanisms under automated policy rules.

\subsection{Summary of Key Contributions}

The primary contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Decoupled Control Plane Architecture:} We propose FedGuard, a governance control plane that orchestrates security and privacy primitives under a unified policy framework, decoupling compliance enforcement from underlying ML execution engines (e.g., Flower~\cite{beutel2020flower}).
    \item \textbf{Statutory Policy Mapping Engine:} We design an automated compliance engine mapping security mechanisms (mTLS 1.3, TPM 2.0 / TEE attestation, RDP accountants) to statutory mandates for U.S. HIPAA (\S164.312)~\cite{hipaa2003security} and EU GDPR (Articles 5, 7, 17, 32)~\cite{gdpr2016regulation}.
    \item \textbf{Resolution of Protocol Incompatibilities:} We resolve the operational conflict between Secure Aggregation tensor masking and Byzantine distance metrics by formalizing dynamic governance mode negotiation (\texttt{SECURE\_AGGREGATION} vs. \texttt{BYZANTINE\_RESILIENT}) with hash-chained audit logging.
    \item \textbf{Compliance-Driven Workflow Orchestration:} We operationalize GDPR Article 17 (Right to Erasure) by pairing client credential eviction with automated machine unlearning (\texttt{FedEraser} checkpoint purification~\cite{liu2021federaser}).
    \item \textbf{Reference Implementation \& Benchmarking:} We present Conclave, a prototype reference implementation integrated with Flower, evaluating deep learning workloads (ResNet-18 on non-IID CIFAR-10) to demonstrate that governance orchestration imposes only 7.2\% runtime overhead.
\end{itemize}
