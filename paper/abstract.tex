Federated Learning (FL) enables collaborative machine learning across distributed, privacy-sensitive enterprise nodes (e.g., healthcare institutions and financial entities) without centralizing raw datasets. However, enterprise deployment of FL faces significant regulatory, security, and operational challenges: enforcing HIPAA and GDPR regulatory compliance, preserving mathematical privacy guarantees, mitigating Byzantine gradient poisoning attacks, and auditing data access trails without compromising node autonomy. Existing FL frameworks prioritize model throughput while treating access control, differential privacy accounting, and auditability as ad-hoc external components.

In this paper, we present \textbf{Conclave}, a comprehensive governance control plane for enterprise federated learning. Conclave seamlessly integrates regulatory compliance enforcement, multi-tenant role-based access control (RBAC/ABAC), tamper-evident audit logging, and automated policy validation with privacy-preserving training pipelines. Cryptographically, Conclave incorporates an X25519-based threshold Secure Aggregation ($k$-of-$N$ Shamir Secret Sharing) protocol resilient to node dropouts and a formal Rényi Differential Privacy (RDP) accountant that persists cumulative $(\epsilon, \delta)$ privacy budget consumption across multi-session training runs in a database. Furthermore, Conclave provides server-side robust aggregation (Trimmed Mean, Coordinate Median, Krum) paired with real-time update divergence anomaly detection to automatically demote and isolate Byzantine adversarial nodes.

We empirically evaluate Conclave on production deep learning workloads, including a PyTorch ResNet-18 vision model (11.17M parameters) on CIFAR-10 data distributions and non-IID Dirichlet label skews ($\alpha \in \{0.1..10\}$). Experimental results demonstrate that Conclave incurs linear, bounded computational overhead ($\approx 1.02$\,s SecAgg latency for 11.17M parameter payloads), enforces 100\% HIPAA/GDPR readiness scores, automatically halts training upon privacy budget exhaustion, and restores global model accuracy from 12\% (unprotected FedAvg) to 84.8\% under 30\% Byzantine poisoning attacks.
